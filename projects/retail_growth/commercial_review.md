# Commercial review framework

The original consultancy compared two competitors across seven dimensions. This reusable framework explains how that comparison supports a sales decision without republishing private client material or inventing competitor operating metrics.

| Dimension | What to capture | Decision supported |
|---|---|---|
| Product range | Categories, complementary products, existing sets | Which combinations or gaps warrant testing? |
| Pricing | Comparable pack sizes, visible prices, discount conditions | Is the proposed comparison like for like? |
| Brand narrative | Origin, craftsmanship, product-use explanations | Can customers understand the value proposition? |
| Shipping | Delivery costs, thresholds, geography | Where might delivered value or purchase friction differ? |
| Events and experiences | Workshops, demonstrations, corporate events | Could an existing capability create another acquisition route? |
| Sales channels | Direct retail, marketplace, wholesale, physical retail | Which channel fits product, economics and operating capacity? |
| Website navigation | Categories, filters, gift discovery, product information | What changes could help shoppers find and choose products? |

Record the URL and observation date when applying this framework to live competitors. Keep visible observations separate from hypotheses about customer behavior. A difference in navigation or storytelling is not itself a measured conversion gap.

## Turning analysis into a sales plan

1. Link a product opportunity to a concrete use case such as gifting.
2. Check whether navigation and content make that use case discoverable.
3. Select an acquisition channel that fits the brand and fulfillment capacity.
4. Define the proposed change, intended audience and measurement period.
5. Test net incremental revenue and margin, with returns and stock-outs as guardrails.

The repository supplies calculations and a demonstration plan; it does not claim these changes were launched or produced a verified sales uplift.
